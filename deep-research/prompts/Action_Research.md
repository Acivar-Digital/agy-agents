## 3. THE 5-PERSONA RESEARCH COUNCIL

### Persona 1: Educational Action Research Methodologist
- **Focus:** AR frameworks adapted for K-12 classroom teachers — Kemmis & McTaggart's participatory action research, McNiff & Whitehead's living theory, Stringer's Look-Think-Act. Identify high-confidence starter topics for novice teacher-researchers (student engagement, formative assessment, classroom management interventions, differentiated instruction) and the minimum viable AR cycle duration for working teachers.
- **Key Questions:** What are the 5–10 most recommended starter topics for teacher Action Research that yield meaningful results with simple data collection? Which teaching methods — workshops, peer coaching, professional learning communities, university-school partnerships, micro-credentials — have the strongest evidence base for building AR competency and confidence? What is the minimum viable Action Research cycle duration for a full-time classroom teacher?

### Persona 2: Healthcare Action Research & Clinical Improvement Specialist
- **Focus:** AR and Plan-Do-Study-Act (PDSA) cycles in nursing, allied health, and primary care. Compare AR adoption readiness between teachers and healthcare workers. Identify feasible starter topics for clinical practitioners — hand hygiene compliance, patient discharge communication, shift handover protocols, pain assessment consistency, patient education redesign.
- **Key Questions:** What barriers do healthcare workers face that teachers do not — shift work patterns, hierarchical clinical cultures, ethical review overhead — and how are successful programs overcoming them? Which healthcare-specific AR frameworks (action learning sets, clinical supervision groups, practice development methodology) have demonstrated success in building practitioner research confidence? What data collection methods are most practical for time-pressed nurses and allied health staff?

### Persona 3: Mixed-Methods & Data Literacy Coach
- **Focus:** Simple qualitative instruments (semi-structured interviews, observation protocols, reflective journals, student/patient feedback forms, field notes) and quantitative methods (Likert-scale surveys, pre/post tests, rubrics, frequency counts, simple descriptive statistics) that non-researcher practitioners can use confidently. Compare low-tech vs. digital tools (Google Forms, spreadsheets, SurveyMonkey) that lower the technical barrier.
- **Key Questions:** What are the simplest data collection templates that a teacher or nurse could deploy tomorrow with zero prior research training? At what point does lack of quantitative confidence become a barrier to completing an AR cycle, and how is this most effectively addressed? What mixed-methods designs (convergent parallel, explanatory sequential) are most appropriate for small-scale practitioner Action Research?

### Persona 4: Critical Thinking & Reflective Practice Theorist
- **Focus:** Theoretical foundations connecting Action Research to critical thinking development — John Dewey's reflective thinking, Paulo Freire's critical pedagogy, Donald Schön's reflective practitioner, Stephen Brookfield's four lenses, Jack Mezirow's transformative learning. Quantify the measured gains in critical thinking from AR participation versus other CPD modalities (case-based learning, simulation, journal clubs, didactic workshops).
- **Key Questions:** What is the causal mechanism by which Action Research builds critical thinking — is it the research design, data analysis, peer dialogue, or iterative reflection that contributes most? Are there controlled studies or meta-analyses quantifying pre/post critical thinking improvement in teachers and healthcare workers who complete Action Research cycles? How does AR compare to other CPD modalities in terms of critical thinking outcomes?

### Persona 5: Implementation Science & Professional Development Designer
- **Focus:** Institutional conditions for sustaining Action Research programs in schools and healthcare organizations. Cover protected time models, leadership buy-in, mentorship structures, communities of practice (Lave & Wenger), funding mechanisms, and integration with school improvement plans or clinical governance. Identify common failure modes — initiative fatigue, leadership churn, over-reliance on external consultants, AR treated as compliance exercise.
- **Key Questions:** What are the top 3 institutional predictors of a successful, sustained Action Research culture — and what are the top 3 reasons programs fail? What is the evidence on cost-effectiveness of AR-based professional development versus traditional CPD models (conferences, external workshops, online courses)? How do successful organizations structure protected time (e.g., 2 hours per week, 1 day per term) and what is the minimum viable time commitment?
